# Processing

These pieces share a common apparatus (admissibility, distinction, repair, and reconstruction) but each one takes that apparatus somewhere different: machine learning, cosmological world-building, punctuation, law, security engineering, and the philosophy of boundaries. Most were written as connective essays, meaning they cite an existing result rather than re-deriving it, then push it into new territory.

## The Essays

### [Repair, Not Reconstruction](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/repair-not-reconstruction.pdf)
*What "Is One Layer Enough?" Shows About the Structure of RL Adaptation*

A close reading of a recent reinforcement-learning paper showing that a single transformer layer can match full-parameter training. Formalizes "repair sets," a way of describing which changes to a pretrained model improve performance while preserving what already works, and argues the paper's uneven layer contributions are evidence of a narrower, depth-structured space of admissible updates.

### [Restoration Before Explanation](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/restoration-before-explanation.pdf)
*Why Systems Can Be Repaired Before They Are Understood*

Argues that repair and explanation are separable operations, drawing on results from two other papers in this program rather than re-deriving them, and works through the same pattern across reinforcement learning, embedded and spacecraft systems, biological healing, and transformer interpretability.

### [Boundaries Are Computational Objects](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/boundaries-computational-objects.pdf)
*Screening, Pursuit, and Refusal as Three Theories of What a Boundary Does*

Compares three theories of what a boundary is: Friston's Markov blankets (statistical screening), Levin's bioelectric boundaries (developmental target-pursuit), and an admissibility-based account of refusal (deletion from a space of permitted trajectories). Argues these are genuinely different mechanisms, not the same idea in different vocabularies, and identifies one specific point of formal disagreement between refusal and free-energy minimization.

### [The Economics of Irreversibility](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/economics-of-irreversibility.pdf)

Derives the accumulation dynamics of long-run "measurement debt" from the cost of a single irreversible event, then extends the result into computing (Landauer's principle), engineering (systems built for restorability rather than explicability), and law (contracts, statutes of limitation, expungement). Flags one open gap in the correspondence rather than papering over it.

### [The Compression Fallacy](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/compression-fallacy.pdf)
*Why Shorter Is Not the Same as Better Understood*

Challenges the common assumption, in both classical MDL theory and contemporary interpretability work, that a shorter description of something means better understanding of it. Distinguishes coding-length deficit from distinguishing-capacity deficit, and argues that engineered simplicity (in pedagogy, construction, or machine learning) is not evidence that the simplified version is closer to the truth.

### [Admissibility Certificates](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/admissibility-certificates.pdf)

Generalizes admissibility and reconstruction-repair theory beyond geometry into a framework for trust in adaptive systems, using an AI-security case study as an empirical anchor. Introduces system histories as the primary object of analysis (rather than instantaneous state), a hierarchy of recovery from rollback through reconstruction to repair, and a resolution of the regress problem in certifying a certificate's own trustworthiness.

### [Parenthetical Asides](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/parenthetical-asides.pdf)
*Nested Scope in Conversation, Games, Stories, and Music*

Starts from a small observation, that punctuation is a low-bitrate encoding of a continuous prosodic signal in speech, and expands it into a general account of scope: how contexts get opened, nested, and closed across conversation, terminal sessions, code, video games, storytelling, and music.

### [The Elements of Computational Worlds](https://standardgalactic.github.io/calculus/processing/computational_worlds.pdf)
*Building a 4X Civilization Simulator from First Principles in Spherepop*

A from-first-principles textbook on building a persistent, rule-governed 4X strategy world out of computational primitives, in the style of Nisan and Schocken's *Elements of Computing Systems*. Ascends from signals and logic gates through memory, tiles, and agents to economies, diplomacy, and history, all implemented in the Spherepop calculus.
