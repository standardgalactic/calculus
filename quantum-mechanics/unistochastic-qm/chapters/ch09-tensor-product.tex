\chapter{The Tensor Product as the Forced Composition Rule}
\label{ch:tensor-product}

Chapter~\ref{ch:local-tomography} showed that the \emph{dimensions} of
composite and product Hermitian state spaces match exactly for $\C$ and fail
to match for $\R,\Hset$. That leaves open whether the composite operator
space for $\C$ is actually \emph{spanned} by products of local operators —
i.e.\ whether the tensor product construction is not merely dimensionally
consistent but the concrete realization of local tomography. This chapter
proves that it is, and exhibits explicitly what goes missing for $\R$.

\section{Local Bases and Their Products}

Fix an $\R$-basis $\{\sigma_i\}_{i=1}^{\beta_\C(d)}$ of the space of
$d\times d$ complex Hermitian matrices $\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d)$ (e.g.\ the
identity together with the generalized Gell-Mann matrices). For a Hermitian
$X$, $X = \tfrac12(X+X^\dagger) $ trivially, so every complex $d\times d$
matrix decomposes uniquely as $H_1 + iH_2$ with $H_1,H_2 \in
\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d)$; consequently $\{\sigma_i\}$, an $\R$-basis of
$\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d)$, is also a $\C$-basis of the full matrix algebra
$M_d(\C)$.

\begin{theorem}[Tensor products of local bases span the composite]
\label{thm:tensor-spans}
\index{Tensor products of local bases span the composite (theorem)}
Let $\{\sigma_i\}_{i=1}^{d_A^2}$, $\{\tau_j\}_{j=1}^{d_B^2}$ be $\R$-bases of
$\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d_A)$, $\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d_B)$. Then $\{\sigma_i \otimes
\tau_j\}$ is an $\R$-basis of $\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d_Ad_B)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each $\sigma_i\otimes\tau_j$ is Hermitian, since $(\sigma_i\otimes\tau_j)^\dagger
= \sigma_i^\dagger \otimes \tau_j^\dagger = \sigma_i\otimes\tau_j$. By the
preceding remark, $\{\sigma_i\}$ and $\{\tau_j\}$ are $\C$-bases of
$M_{d_A}(\C)$ and $M_{d_B}(\C)$ respectively; since $M_{d_A}(\C)\otimes_\C
M_{d_B}(\C) \cong M_{d_Ad_B}(\C)$ via the Kronecker product (a standard fact
about matrix algebras), $\{\sigma_i\otimes\tau_j\}$ is a $\C$-basis of
$M_{d_Ad_B}(\C)$: in particular $\C$-linearly independent, hence also
$\R$-linearly independent (an $\R$-linear dependence is a special case of a
$\C$-linear one). This gives $d_A^2 d_B^2 = \beta_\C(d_Ad_B)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:local-tomography}) $\R$-linearly independent Hermitian
elements inside the real vector space $\mathrm{Herm}_\C(d_Ad_B)$, which has
exactly that real dimension; they therefore form a basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This upgrades Chapter~\ref{ch:local-tomography}'s dimension count to a
concrete claim: for $\C$, \emph{every} composite observable is a real-linear
combination of tensor products of local observables. Local tomography for
$\C$ is not a coincidence of arithmetic; the tensor product construction is
its literal witness.
\end{remark}

\section{What Goes Missing for \texorpdfstring{$\R$}{R}: an Explicit Example}

The same construction over $\R$ shows exactly where local tomography fails.
Take $d_A=d_B=2$. A real basis of $\mathrm{Herm}_\R(2)$ (real symmetric
$2\times2$ matrices) is $\{I,X,Z\}$ with $X=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0
\end{psmallmatrix}$, $Z=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}$ —
only $3 = \beta_\R(2)$ elements, since the fourth generator needed to span
all of $M_2(\R)$,
\begin{equation}
J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
is \emph{antisymmetric} ($J^\top=-J$), not a valid local observable over
$\R$. (Chapter~\ref{ch:amplitwist} gives $J$'s full elementary derivation as
the generator of quarter-turns and shows it is, identically, the real
matrix representing $i$ throughout this book and in the recent literature
discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:final-audit}.)

\begin{remark}
$J$ should not be confused with the complex Pauli matrix $\sigma_y =
\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, which \emph{is} Hermitian
over $\C$ and belongs to $\mathrm{Herm}_\C(2)$. $J$ is the real,
scalar-stripped matrix $-i\sigma_y$; it is a perfectly good real
\emph{antisymmetric} matrix, just not a real \emph{symmetric} one, and
therefore not itself an admissible local observable in the real theory of
Chapter~\ref{ch:local-tomography}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:JJ-missing}
The nine products $\{I,X,Z\}\otimes\{I,X,Z\}$ are linearly independent
elements of $\mathrm{Herm}_\R(4)$, but do not span it: $J\otimes J$ is
symmetric,
\begin{equation}
(J\otimes J)^\top = J^\top \otimes J^\top = (-J)\otimes(-J) = J\otimes J,
\end{equation}
hence lies in $\mathrm{Herm}_\R(4)$, and is linearly independent of the nine
products above.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linear independence of the nine products, and of $J\otimes J$ from them,
follows because $\{I,X,Z,J\}$ is a full basis of $M_2(\R)$ (dimension $4 =
2^2$), so $\{I,X,Z,J\}\otimes\{I,X,Z,J\}$ (sixteen elements) is a basis of
$M_4(\R)$ (dimension $16=4^2$) by the same Kronecker-product-of-bases fact
used in Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-spans}; $J\otimes J$ is one of these sixteen
basis elements, hence independent of the other fifteen, in particular the
nine drawn only from $\{I,X,Z\}\otimes\{I,X,Z\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the exact real tenth dimension counted abstractly in
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-tomography} ($\beta_\R(4)=10$ vs.\ $\beta_\R(2)^2=9$),
now identified concretely. $J$ is not a locally realizable real observable —
it is antisymmetric — yet $J\otimes J$ \emph{is} a legitimate real symmetric
observable on the composite system, invisible to any measurement scheme built
only from products of local real observables. Real composite systems carry a
genuinely nonlocal degree of freedom built from two individually-inadmissible
local generators whose product happens to be admissible; this is the concrete
mechanism behind Chapter~\ref{ch:local-tomography}'s abstract dimension
mismatch, not merely a restatement of it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Quaternionic overcompleteness]
The mirror phenomenon occurs for $\Hset$, in the opposite direction: the
analogous product basis is \emph{overcomplete}
($\beta_\Hset(d_A)\beta_\Hset(d_B) > \beta_\Hset(d_Ad_B)$ for $d_A,d_B\ge2$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-tomography}), so the products $\{\sigma_i\otimes
\tau_j\}$ must satisfy nontrivial linear relations rather than failing to
span. This is consistent with the well-documented difficulty of defining a
well-behaved, associative tensor product for quaternionic Hilbert
spaces~\cite{adler1995}; this book does not attempt to resolve that
difficulty, only to note that it is exactly where the dimension count
predicts trouble.
\end{remark}

\begin{ledgerentry}[End of Chapter~\ref{ch:tensor-product}]
\textbf{Established:} for $\C$, tensor products of local Hermitian bases do
not merely match the composite dimension but literally span the composite
Hermitian operator space (Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-spans}); for $\R$, the
missing dimension is identified concretely as spanned by $J\otimes J$, a
product of two locally-inadmissible generators
(Proposition~\ref{prop:JJ-missing}).\\
\textbf{Not established:} the positivity cone, normalization, or Born-rule
structure of the composite state space — this chapter concerns only the
ambient \emph{linear} space of Hermitian operators, which observables and
states must live inside but does not by itself determine which elements are
valid (positive, normalized) states. Entanglement, the tensor-product Born
rule $p_i = \operatorname{tr}(\rho E_i)$ for composite measurements, and the
partial trace remain to be established in later chapters of the recovery
layer.
\end{ledgerentry}
